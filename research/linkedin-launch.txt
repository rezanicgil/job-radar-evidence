A job asks for three years of C++. Your CV documents two and a half. What would a single “match score” tell you to change?

I built Job Radar Evidence to give applicants something they can act on. Pick a requirement from a Turkish or English job posting, see the relevant passages from your CV, and decide what the evidence supports. Your CV stays in your browser.

In the demo, a fictional candidate documents 30 of the 36 months requested for C++ and fully demonstrates Python test reporting. The report shows 1 of 2 selected requirements supported (50%) and identifies the six months that still need evidence. That percentage describes the CV, not the candidate’s chances of getting hired.

I fine-tuned a multilingual retriever on 1,027 English–Turkish documentation pairs. It found five more correct translations at rank one on 721 held-out pairs, but on a separate check with ten job conditions, none of the target CV passages moved up. That’s why applicants see the source passages and make the final call.

The project also tracks software jobs in Türkiye. On November 10, 2026, all 152 selected technical postings had readable descriptions; only 65 had qualification headings we could measure. You can inspect the denominator before interpreting a technology percentage.
