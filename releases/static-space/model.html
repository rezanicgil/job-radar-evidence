Turkish preference wording

We trained the final encoder layer and classification head on Turkish job bullets from one employer and tested on the other, withholding headings and deduplicating each employer’s text. In both directions, the model classified every test bullet as required. It missed all 35 CRENNO preferences and both Roof Stacks preferences. Searching for explicit preference phrases recovered 6/35 and 2/2 respectively. These references come from employer sections and explicit wording. English mobile job conditions

Across seven mobile job URLs from two employers, 114 source references yielded 40 distinct English bullets after overlap checks. With headings hidden, the live sentence model retrieved 21 of 23 bullets from qualification and preference sections and selected one benefits bullet. A SkillSpan encoder selected one of 23. One additional training epoch on 390 weakly labelled employer bullets raised this to four of 23, with seven benefits bullets selected. Does the stated experience belong to the applicant?

For four technical roles we compared three fictional CV passages. One states the applicant’s hands-on experience, one attributes the duration to another technology and one describes a different responsibility. The relevant passage can reveal that the applicant has too little experience.

Across 16 English and Turkish comparisons, BM25 ranked the relevant passage first in 5 (31%). The multilingual base model, the released fine-tuned retriever and a new contrastive candidate each did so in 1 (6%). Each of the four scenarios appears in four language combinations. Bilingual passage retrieval

A separate encoder was fine-tuned on 1,027 English–Turkish Python documentation pairs. On ten other documents, English queries retrieved the correct Turkish translation first in 674 of 721 cases, up from 669 with the base model. On ten job conditions searched against 30 fictional CV passages, all ten target passages kept their original rank.

Can the first result be a colleague’s work? In four additional conditions with three plausible passages each, the translation encoder ranked the supporting passage first for 1/4 English requirements paired with Turkish CV excerpts and 0/4 Turkish requirements paired with English excerpts. A classifier trained on who performed the work ranked it first for 2/4 in each direction. The passages are fictional and the reference labels are project-authored. External evaluation

On 1,759 external CV/job pairs, the fine-tuned encoder agreed with the ordering of differently labelled jobs for the same applicant 64.58% of the time, averaged over 275 CVs. The base encoder reached 63.25%. The source labels refer to whole documents and their reviewer provenance is undocumented. Inspect both ranking directions
